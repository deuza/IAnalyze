=== TOOLS ===

Two cases, according to what you really have. State which one you are
in, in one line: the first line of your answer, before the recap asked
in START. Never claim to have run a tool that you do not have, and make
up no result of an execution.

If you run code but the installation of python-chess fails, for lack of
network access for example, you are in case B: say so, and follow

CASE A, you can run code (sandbox, code interpreter):

Install python-chess there (pip install chess, in a venv if the system
requires it).
Stockfish is useless: section 3 provides the evaluations.

Build the annotated PGN with python-chess (Game objects, variations,
comments, NAGs) and export it with StringExporter, rather than writing
it by hand: the legality of each move, variations included, and the
structure are thus guaranteed. Three verified behaviors of
python-chess:
- StringExporter wraps the lines of moves, but never the inside of a
  comment. So wrap each comment yourself at 75 columns (textwrap.fill)
  before assigning it: the inner line breaks are kept. Not more: the
  export adds a brace and a space on each side, and a comment of a
  single full line then reaches 79 characters.
- The comment placed before move 1 is game.comment.
- The tags other than the seven mandatory ones come out in their order
  of insertion: insert them in alphabetical order.
Then reload the file with chess.pgn.read_game, and check that
game.errors is empty and that no line exceeds 79 characters.

CASE B, you cannot run code:

CONSTRAINTS. Nothing then checks your moves for you:
- copy the main line of section 1 without changing it;
- replay each variation move by move from the position before the move

- prefer the lines of section 3a or the pv_san variations of
  stockfish/evaluations.json, already legal;
- wrap each line yourself before 80 characters;
- leave no blank line in the body of the game, and write each variation

=== OVERVIEW OF THE GAME (before move 1) ===

A single comment, of 6 to 10 lines, holding:
- the opening played and its guiding idea;
- the story of the game in three sentences;
- the turning point, with its move number;
- the cause of the decision, what really tipped the game;
- the theme that the reader must remember.

=== MOVE COMMENTS ===

Comment on BOTH sides, not only the player.

Do not comment on every move: an ordinary developing move deserves
nothing. Focus on:
- the turning points, the mistakes and the missed chances;
- the ends of phases (leaving the opening, middlegame, switch to the
  endgame), helped by the phase markers of section 1;
- the moves that change the nature of the position (pawn structure,
  opening of a file, decisive exchange).

Aim for 15 to 25 commented moves over a whole game, 5 to 10 over the
first 15 moves. Any move classified as a mistake or a blunder, a loss
of at least {{MISTAKE_THRESHOLD}} cp, must be commented on; if these moves exceed the
target, the obligation prevails.

Content of each comment, as applicable:
- the INTENT of the move: which plan it serves;
- the serious ALTERNATIVE, with its variation in notation;
- the TACTICS: motif present or avoided (pin, fork, skewer, deflection,
  decoy, X-ray, intermediate move, underpromotion);
- the STRATEGY: pawn structure, weak squares, open files, bishop pair,
  activity of the pieces, safety of the kings, long-term plan;
- the EVALUATION of the position obtained, in plain words AND with a
  NAG.

After a mistake, always say what should have been played and WHY, with
the variation, starting from the first choice of the engine and its
line: sections 3a and 3b.

=== NAGs (numeric annotation glyphs) ===

Place the NAG right after the move, separated by a space.

Quality of the move (values 1 to 8):
  $1 = !    good move           $2 = ?    poor move
  $3 = !!   excellent move      $4 = ??   blunder
  $5 = !?   interesting move    $6 = ?!   dubious move
  $7 = forced move              $8 = only playable move

Default mapping from the Category column: "inaccuracy" $6,
"mistake" $2, "blunder" $4. Your judgment may depart from it, for
example for a harmless blunder in a position already lost.

$7 and $8 go on the move that had no alternative, the forced reply,
never on the move that forces it, a check for instance.

Evaluation of the position (values 10 to 21):
  $10 = drawish position             $13 = unclear position
  $14 = += slight advantage White    $15 = =+ slight advantage Black
  $16 = +/- clear advantage White    $17 = -/+ clear advantage Black
  $18 = +- decisive advantage White  $19 = -+ decisive advantage Black
  $20 = ++- crushing, Black should resign
  $21 = --+ crushing, White should resign

Useful thematic positions:
  $22 / $23 = White / Black in zugzwang
  $36 / $37 = initiative for White / Black
  $40 / $41 = attack for White / Black
  $44 / $45 = sufficient compensation for the material, White / Black
  $132 / $133 = moderate counterplay for White / Black
  $138 / $139 = severe time trouble for White / Black

Do NOT use the suffix notation (Qxa8?): it belongs to the import
format. Use the NAG (Qxa8 $2).

=== VARIATIONS ===

The variations go between parentheses, right after the move they
replace. They may be nested. Give the move number and the ellipsis for
a Black move: (12... Nd4 $1 {threatens Nxe2+}).

A variation is written outside any comment. The order is always: the
move, its NAG, its comment between braces, then the variation between
parentheses, and the game resumes:

  11. Ne4 $4 { Your knight is hanging. } ( 11. dxc5 Bc7 12. Nb3 )
  11... dxe4

#! note On 25 November 2026, an LLM put ( 19... Kg8 20. Qh7# ) after
#! note 19. Qh5+, the continuation actually played: illegal there,
#! note the variation made python-chess stop the main line.
A variation replaces the move it follows, here 11. Ne4: it starts from
the position before that move, with the same side to move: it begins
with 11. dxc5, never with 11... dxe4. The moves actually played never
go between parentheses: the game resumes after the variation.

Never write the variation inside the braces: between braces, a sequence
of moves is only text, which the reader cannot replay on the board.

Keep to 3 to 5 plies per variation: beyond that, the reader loses
track.

=== FORMAT CONSTRAINTS, MANDATORY ===

1. BRACES: never write a brace in a comment. python-chess silently
   removes any closing brace anyway, which would mutilate the text.
2. SEMICOLON: avoid it inside a comment.
3. LINE LENGTH: fewer than 80 characters per line, comments included,
   hence the wrapping at 75 columns described in TOOLS.
4. PERCENT: no % character in the comments, write "percent". A line
   break could put it in the first column, where the standard gives it
   an escape role.
5. NOTATION: standard algebraic notation (K Q R B N, no letter for a
   pawn). Castling: O-O and O-O-O, with the capital letter O, never the
   digit zero.
6. RESULT: the final marker is identical to the Result tag.
7. LEGALITY: no illegal move, variations included. In case A,
   building with python-chess and reloading, as described in TOOLS,
   prove it: mention their result in one line. In case B, say in one
   line that the legality was checked by hand, without a tool, and that
   the player must open the file to confirm it.
8. ENCODING: UTF-8. If SCID shows wrong characters, the player will
   re-encode the file in ISO-8859-1 on their side.
9. BLANK LINES: a single one, between the tags and the body of the
   game. None in the body, not even after the overview before move 1:
   a PGN reader sees there the end of the game and does not read what
   follows.
